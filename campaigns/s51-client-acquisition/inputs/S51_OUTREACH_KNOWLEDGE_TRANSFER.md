## 6. Anti-AI writing rules (hard constraints for every email)

**Banned words** (replace with the simple alternative): delve, intricate, tapestry, pivotal, underscore, landscape, foster, testament, enhance, crucial, multifaceted, comprehensive, leverage (verb), robust, seamless, synergy, transformative, unwavering, vibrant, nuanced, paradigm, realm, harness, spearhead, cornerstone, elevate, embark, resonate, captivate, beacon, bustling.

**Banned phrases:** "It's important to note," "It's worth noting," "In today's [anything]," "serves as a testament," "stands as a," "plays a vital/crucial role."

**Banned transitions to open sentences:** Moreover, Furthermore, Notably, Additionally (unless the logic genuinely requires the connector).

**Banned structures:**
1. Negative parallelism ("It's not X, it's Y" / "Not only… but also").
2. Rule of threes (don't force every list to exactly three items — use two, or four).
3. Em dashes.
4. Compulsive summaries.
5. Superficial -ing closers (…ensuring greater efficiency).
6. False ranges ("From startups to enterprises…").
7. Bolded term + restatement (**Scalability:** the ability to scale…).
8. Inflated symbolism (make it a specific fact instead).
9. Vague attribution ("Industry experts suggest…", "Studies show…") — name the source or cut it.
10. Formatting overkill (no emoji, no bolding every other phrase).

**Do instead:** simplest word that fits · vary list length · let sentences end · cut interchangeable sentences · replace adjectives with facts · write like S51, not like an assistant.

## 7. Cold-email craft rules (the heart of personalization)

### The reader you're writing to
A C-suite / founder / MD / PE partner who reads 200+ inbound emails a week and discards 95% in the first sentence, has been pitched every category of SaaS, and decides whether to keep reading based on whether sentence 1 shows you understand **their job** — not your product. **If the first sentence doesn't earn the second, the email is dead.** The bar: would a peer write this same sentence to them?

### Two Email-1 opener patterns (pick ONE per prospect, document the choice)

**Pattern A — Role-Expectation Opener.** Name the unspoken expectation of their seat — the thing the title implies but the job description never says — then frame S51 as what resolves the gap.
- S1: As [title], the visible thing you're judged on is [X]. The invisible one is [Y — where the brand/narrative either compounds or leaks].
- S2: The specific tension that lives in that gap, in *their* vocabulary.
- S3: One short clause placing S51 against the gap (predicate, not subject).
- S4: A genuine question, not a CTA.
- *Use when:* their role itself is the hook, or their public footprint is thin, or their title is the unusual part.
- *Don't:* literally reuse the words "visible/invisible" every time; vary the framing.

**Pattern B — Verified Observation Opener.** Open with a specific, sourced fact about their company/work/decision/recent moment, then pivot to the system question it raises.
- S1: A specific, verifiable observation (an account win, a service launch, a funding round, a published interview point). Never a generic industry trend.
- S2: The operational implication a peer would see in that fact.
- S3: One short clause anchoring S51 to that implication.
- S4: A genuine question.
- *Use when:* a concrete public fact is the strongest hook (M&A, AI rollout, major account win, fresh raise).
- *Don't:* cite a fact you can't source.

### Sentence-by-sentence discipline
- **S1 must earn S2.** Delete on sight: "I came across your profile," "I hope this finds you well," "Just wanted to reach out," "I noticed you're the [title] at [company]," "As a fellow [role]," "We help companies like yours," "Quick question for you."
- **S2 adds information**, doesn't restate S1.
- **S3 places the product, doesn't pitch it.** S51 appears in one short clause as the predicate. Not: "S51 is the world's first AI-native storytelling agency that helps B2B brands unify…". Instead: "S51 is the extended creative team a few B2B SaaS founders already run their narrative through."
- **S4 is a question, not a calendar link.** Answerable in one sentence. "Worth a short call?" is fine. "Would you be open to a 15-minute discovery next Tue or Thu?" is not.

### AI tells to avoid (even though the words aren't "banned")
- "Most [role]s in your seat tell us the same thing…" (vague attribution)
- "The recurring question we hear from X is…"
- "From the [role] seat at a [company type]…" (formulaic merge)
- "Worth a short call?" in *every* email (vary the closing question)
- "Following up on…" when there's nothing to follow up on
- "Genuine question, no pitch attached" (performing genuineness signals the opposite)
- Reflexive three-item lists
- The prospect's name alone on its own line as the opener

### Two tests to run on every email
1. **Voice Diff Test:** replace every prospect name/company mention with `[NAME]`/`[COMPANY]` and re-read. If the rest still works for any other prospect of the same role, the personalization is a sticker — rewrite so the **logic** is personal, not just the facts.
2. **First-Sentence Test:** delete sentence 1 and re-read. If it still makes sense, sentence 1 was AI filler — rewrite the opener.

### Personalization hooks — real vs. decorative
A **real** hook changes the email's argument (a company fact that creates the operational tension the email is about; a role structure the argument depends on; a recent decision with known operational implications). A **decorative** hook is a fact dropped in to prove you researched ("I noticed you went to [school]," "Congrats on the role change," "Your post resonated"). If removing the hook doesn't change the argument, cut it.

### Sender voice
- **Default sender: Daniel Raj, Founder, S51.** Writes founder-to-founder / peer-to-peer. No deferential openings ("Hope I'm not intruding"). Founders don't sell — they name a problem and ask if it's worth a conversation. Plain sign-off: "Dan / Daniel Raj, Founder, S51 Agency."
- If using a **dual-sender hand-off** (e.g., Dan → an account lead for Email 5): Email 4 names the second sender by name and role; Email 5 opens referencing Dan without re-explaining the setup; keep the two voices distinct.

### Subject lines
- Max 9 words, sentence case (not Title Case), no emoji, no all-caps.
- Subject A and B test **different angles**, not different wordings of one angle. One specific to their company/role; one specific to a tension.
- **Out:** "[Topic] + [vague benefit]", "Quick question, [Name]", "[Company] x S51", "Re: [topic]" with no prior thread.
- **In (style):** "The narrative under [their product]", "[Company] and the authority-gap question", "Growth exists, authority doesn't".

## 9. Personalization brief (fill this BEFORE drafting any sequence)

For each prospect, complete a brief and source every fact. Citation format: `[L1: where on their LinkedIn]`, `[L2: their company's official site/press]`, `[L3: trade press — outlet, headline, date]`. Unverifiable → `[VERIFY]`, keep out of the body.

**Sections to fill:**
1. **Prospect profile** — name, exact title, exact company entity, reports to, tenure, location, LinkedIn URL, activity level (Active/Semi/Dormant). `[L1]`
2. **Scope of current role** — 3–5 bullets in their own words from their About/Experience. No paraphrasing. `[L1]`
3. **Verified career arc** — reverse-chronological, LinkedIn-confirmed only. `[L1]`
4. **Company context** — parent group, hierarchy, offices in scope, recent M&A, leadership announcements, revenue/scale, client roster they're tied to, awards (12 mo), recent product/AI announcements. `[L2 + L3]`
5. **Tier + pain theme assignment** — which of the five tiers (§3) and which single pain theme the email will argue.
6. **Email-1 opener pattern** — A or B, with a one-sentence reason.
7. **Personalization hooks** — each must pass the "if I remove this fact, does the argument change?" test.
8. **Operational implication** — one paragraph bridging the hook to S51, in *their* vocabulary.
9. **The ONE S51 offer/capability** that resolves the implication (§2). Name one, not a list.
10. **Capabilities to NOT surface** — 2–3 that would dilute the email.
11. **LinkedIn touchpoint plan** — Day -3 view / Day -2 connect (note A + B) / Day 0 email.
12. **Sender** — Dan (default), and second sender if a hand-off is used.
13. **Language/tone note** — reading culture (UK/US/EU/APAC/MENA), idiom risks.
14. **Fact-verification audit log** — every claim that will appear in the emails, with L1/L2/L3 sources. Claims with fewer than the required sources get pulled or downgraded to `[VERIFY]`.

**Source layers for S51 outreach:**
- **L1 = the prospect's own LinkedIn** (About, Experience, Featured posts, their articles). Third-party scrapers (RocketReach, ZoomInfo) are L3 at best.
- **L2 = the prospect's company official sources** (their site, About/News pages, official press releases).
- **L3 = trade press** (Campaign, Adweek, Ad Age, The Drum, LBB, TechCrunch/industry press for their vertical, plus local-market outlets).
- **Triangulation:** claims about the person's own scope → L1 alone OK; company positioning/footprint → L2 alone OK; industry milestones (award, raise, M&A) → L3, and if the claim is about scale/acquisitions/strategic moves, require **two of three** layers.
- **LinkedIn 403 caveat:** if you can only get a WebSearch summary (not the live profile), treat it as L3, mark the row `L1: PENDING manual confirmation`, and don't send until a human confirms L1.

## 10. Mandatory QC before any sequence ships (3 gates)

No sequence is delivered or sent until all three gates pass. Each gate writes a `qc-report.md`.

**Gate 1 — Fact Verification.** Every claim in all five emails maps to L1/L2/L3 sources in the audit log (§9). Any claim marked REVISE or REMOVE fails the gate → revise and re-run.

**Gate 2 — Anti-AI Voice Audit** (six tests):
1. Banned vocabulary sweep (§6) — zero tolerance.
2. Banned structures sweep (§6) — negative parallelism, reflexive threes, em dashes, summaries, -ing closers, bold+restate, vague attribution, formulaic openers.
3. Voice Diff Test (§7).
4. First-Sentence Test (§7).
5. Cross-Email Pattern Test — read all five in order; if the same opener pattern repeats, the voice went template — rewrite.
6. Read-Aloud Test — cut every sentence that's prose-for-prose's-sake.

**Gate 3 — C-Suite QC Panel** (three reviewers, each must return APPROVE):
- **Reviewer 1 — Marketing Ops Director (ex-agency, 20+ yrs):** Does Email 1 earn the second sentence? Is the operational logic specific enough a peer recognizes the diagnosis? Anything that reads junior/salesy/under-briefed?
- **Reviewer 2 — Ex-CFO of a holding-group agency:** Are metrics sourced/attributed? Does the financial logic survive a CFO's read? Any number/claim they'd publicly correct?
- **Reviewer 3 — Ex-CCO of a creative network:** Is the tone peer-level and crafted (not bullet-points strung together)? Would they forward it to their CFO/CMO and feel good about the sender?

If any reviewer returns REVISE, fix the specific issues and re-run the panel. For batches of 10+ prospects, run the three reviewers as parallel independent reviews per prospect.

**One-page pre-draft checklist** (answer each in one sentence before writing):
1. What is the unspoken expectation of this prospect's seat?
2. What is the most specific, sourced public fact about their work/company in the last 12 months?
3. What operational implication does that fact create?
4. Which single S51 offer/capability resolves it?
5. What question can they answer in one sentence that tells you the conversation is worth having?

If any answer isn't crisp, you're not ready to draft — go back to the brief.
